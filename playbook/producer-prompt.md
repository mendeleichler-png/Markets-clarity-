## THE SCRIPT (where videos are won or lost)

Channel data is blunt: the share of viewers who *stay* in the first seconds sets the ceiling — ~37% stayed dies at ~1,000 views; 60%+ breaks out. Spend your best craft on frame 0 and the first spoken line.

- **Triple hook, stacked in the first ~2 seconds, all three layers aimed at ONE payoff:** (1) VISUAL — frame 0 is the scene itself: the strongest real moment, face, object or document, legible at phone size — never a title card or decorative backdrop; (2) SPOKEN — the first line names the scene the viewer is looking at AND lands the dollar figure, loss, or contradiction; (3) TEXT — big on-screen text, 2–6 words that sharpen the spoken line (the number, the verdict, the stakes), never a transcript of it.
- **SCENE BEFORE CLAIM (hard rule):** when the hook turns on a physical object, place, or document, the scene is established before or as the claim lands — frame 0 shows it, and the first line situates it ("The tablet flips toward you at the counter…"). A claim about an object the viewer hasn't been shown or situated in is an automatic fail.
- **The 3-Second Stranger Test:** a stranger who sees frame 0 and hears only the first ~3 seconds must be able to answer: What am I looking at? Where are we? What's the question? If any answer is missing, the open fails. Audit the words *that / this / it / they* in the first two lines one by one — each must point at something already visible or named in the same breath. Every name, noun, and number is introduced before it is used.
- **Hook anatomy:** shock → expectations → payoff preview (say what the viewer will know by the end). Write the payoff first, then the hook that promises exactly that payoff — a hook that promises more than the body pays loses the viewer mid-video. The ending must repay the opening.
- **NAME RULE:** the subject's full name is SPOKEN in the narration within the first lines after the cold open. Naming them only in the title does not count.
- **Readability:** 5th-grade level, spoken English, short words, short sentences. One idea per sentence; one sentence per breath; a figure the video turns on gets its own beat, never buried mid-sentence. No insider shorthand — if a term needs prior knowledge, situate it in ≤5 words or cut it. The hook must work with the sound OFF.
- **Structure (35–55s, ~140–165 spoken words, 5 segments):** (1) Scene + hook (0–~7s); (2) Mechanism — how it actually works, in *but / therefore* steps, each a new fact or turn, never a restatement; (3) Proof — the number, document, or moment that proves it, SHOWN on screen, where the hook's loop closes; (4) Verdict — one line that repays the opening and loops back into frame 0. Re-hook at every segment boundary (a new question, reversal, or escalation) — never announce a topic change like a lecture. No new claims in the verdict. No subscribe CTA inside the narration.
- **Ten-pass self-review before you build (mandatory):** run the finished script through ten separate critical passes and fix what they catch: (1) cold-viewer clarity, (2) hook strength, (3) fact precision — every claim re-checked against sources, every number re-computed (this pass has a veto), (4) pace & retention — a new idea every sentence, no flat stretch over ~5 seconds, (5) taste — is there a real moment a general audience would share?, (6) curiosity-gap integrity — every loop opened is closed at the promised point, (7) visual alignment — every spoken claim has a showable visual, no claim about an unseen object anywhere, (8) language & readability, (9) structure & story order — is the best beat buried?, (10) hostile pre-mortem — assume the video died at 900 views; write the exact second a stranger swipes and why, then fix it. Keep the pass notes; they ship with delivery.

## EDITING STANDARD

- **Cuts every 0.7–1.5 seconds** in Shorts; no static hold over 2 seconds; a pattern interrupt (graphic, punch-in, angle change) every ~3 seconds. Real motion footage throughout — never hold a slide or photo still.
- **People and faces in the majority of shots.** Reactions sell; charts alone never carry a video.
- **Framing:** every shot is reframed around its subject — face or product centered, no clipped faces, no half-cut phones. If a crop can't hold the subject, place the footage in a designed window over a branded background instead of cropping to fill.
- **No clip is ever used twice** in one video. Keep a shot log.
- **Captions:** big, kinetic, word-by-word, with numbers and names highlighted. Captions must match the narration exactly.
- **Loop ending:** the last line/frame hands back to the first, so a rewatch feels seamless.

## GRAPHICS STANDARD

- Every major stat gets an **animated** visual: count-up numbers, draw-on charts, growing bars, split-screen comparisons. A data visual at least every 15–20 seconds.
- Graphics **fill the frame**: giant hero numbers, glow/gradient/texture, one visual metaphor per graphic. At phone size it should look like a designed poster. If a document is the hero (a contract, a filing, a court judgment), show the real document, legible.
- **BANNED:** spec-sheet/infobox cards (label:value rows), text-list slides, bullet walls, plain word cards, a small chart floating in an empty dark frame, thin default fonts for hero text, generic finance clichés (robot toys, handshakes, random trading-desk wallpaper). When a beat has no footage, build a sophisticated animated data graphic — never a word card.
- Put a small source line on data graphics where the source isn't already on screen (e.g. "SEC filing, Nov 2026") — it reads as credibility.

## VOICE & SOUND (the channel's signature — match it exactly)

- **Narration voice:** the channel's current standard is **Kokoro TTS, voice "am_onyx"** — a deep, warm, confident American male voice — rendered at about 0.80 speed. If you can run Kokoro, use exactly that voice and setting for consistency. If you cannot, tell me before building, use the closest deep American male voice you have, and say plainly that you substituted. Never use a flat robotic default voice, and never let pitch or pace drift between segments of one video — generate long takes, not stitched fragments.
- **Pace:** measured, human storytelling — about **0.33 seconds per word** on average, with natural variation (a metronome-flat read is a defect).
- **Breath groups:** short — 7–10 words per breath group, never more than ~20. Script the pauses: include deliberate **hold beats of ~1–1.5 seconds** before payoffs. Sentences land with a falling pitch at the end, like a person making a point.
- **Word-level loudness:** even and leveled (no word noticeably louder or quieter than its neighbors).
- **Mix:** energetic royalty-free music bed, ducked to sit **8–13 dB under the voice** — close enough that pauses are never dead air (no silent gap ≥0.4s anywhere). SFX hits on cuts and number reveals, whooshes on graphic entries. Final mix **−14 LUFS**, true peak ≤ −1 dB.
- Narration must be context-complete: every number gets a unit and a comparison; a viewer who can't see any source material must never hit a dangling reference.
