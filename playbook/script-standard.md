# markets clarity — SCRIPT STANDARD (draft, 2026-11-15)

Status: DRAFT for owner review. Written after the owner's 2026-11-15 verdicts (voice + script). Production stays held until he clears a restart; when it restarts, **no script is built until it has passed all ten review passes in §5** and the sign-off checklist in §6 is complete.

Why this exists: the tip-screen Short opened on "That 25% button is lying to you" — a claim about an object the cold viewer had never been shown or situated in. The scene (a tablet flipping toward you at a counter, a line behind you) was in the script — one sentence too late. The gates all passed; the message didn't land. This standard makes that failure class impossible to ship.

## 1. What the research says (sources studied 2026-11-15)

Teaching content studied: Jenny Hoyos with YouTube Shorts product lead Todd Sherman (Creator Insider conversation, Jan 2025) and her TED talk "The secret to telling a great story — in less than 60 seconds" (Oct 2024); Creator Science interviews with Hoyos and with Phill Agnew on MrBeast's psychology; the 37-transcript MrBeast retention analysis (InfluencerNotes, 2026) and reporting on MrBeast's leaked production memo; YouTube Help / Official Blog positions as compiled by CapsAI's hook guide (Oct 2026); practitioner syntheses (Everpop, ViralGen, Blitzcut, OutlierKit scriptwriting comparison).

Distilled, with the channel translation:

1. **The hook is at second zero.** All 37 MrBeast videos hook in the first sentence — no greeting, no channel name, no context before the hook. "The first sentence earns the right to explain." (MrBeast transcript analysis.)
2. **Hoyos's 3-step hook: shock → expectations → payoff preview.** (1) A shock, ideally *visual*; (2) establish what the content is about; (3) state what the viewer gets at the end and how we'll get there. Then *satisfy* — the ending repays the opening. A hook is a debt; the video is the repayment.
3. **Visual first, words second.** Hoyos sketches the visual before writing a line. The hook must be understandable **with the sound off**, simple enough to explain to a 5-year-old, and strong enough to work as a long-form title + thumbnail.
4. **Readability ceiling: 5th grade.** Hoyos runs every script through a readability checker and ships nothing above a 5th-grade level. Short words, short sentences, spoken English — not essay English.
5. **The three layers point at one payoff.** Frame 0 + first spoken line + first on-screen text are one unit aimed at the same payoff (CapsAI/YouTube Help). On-screen text *sharpens* the spoken line; it does not repeat it. The first caption is part of the hook, not a delayed subtitle.
6. **Promise by ~2 seconds; body delivering by ~3.** Write the payoff first, then the hook that promises exactly that payoff. A hook that promises more than the body pays loses the viewer a few seconds later — the drop shows up mid-video, not at the open.
7. **Loops must close.** A curiosity gap works only if the viewer can identify the gap and the video closes it, at the promised time (Zeigarnik/Loewenstein via the MrBeast reporting; ClipCreator's failure mode: "the gap should be the actual value of the video" — never hide a minor detail behind dramatic language).
8. **Every second re-earns the next.** Each sentence adds a new idea, question, or shift (micro-beats). MrBeast's team scripts re-hooks at expected drop-off points and escalates stakes; for a 40–55s Short that means a re-hook or turn roughly every 10–15 seconds, and "whatever you say you're gonna do, you end it right after you do it" (Hoyos) — no padding after a payoff.
9. **Story beats connect with *but / therefore*, not *and then*.** (Hoyos.) A complete mini-story: an immediately understood question → visible progress toward the answer → a turn or obstacle → the payoff that repays the opening.
10. **The metric is "Stayed to watch."** YouTube's own Shorts discovery runs on % who chose to view, average view duration, average % viewed. Our channel data agrees: the stayed rate sets the ceiling (Odyssey 38.2% died at 984 views; Cybertruck 62.1% kept a pulse). Craft budget goes to frame 0 and the first line — but per rule 6, the *script's* first line is where the stayed rate is won or lost.

## 2. Hook anatomy (channel standard)

The opening unit is three layers over one payoff, landing inside ~2 seconds:

- **VISUAL (frame 0):** the *scene itself*, finished and legible at phone size — the actual thing the video is about (the tablet at the counter, the slot machine, the house), not a decorative backdrop and not an abstract graphic. If the video's subject is a physical object or place, frame 0 shows it, situated.
- **SPOKEN (first line):** present tense, active, specific. It names the scene the viewer is looking at and lands the contradiction or question. One sentence. It must make sense to someone who has seen *only* frame 0.
- **TEXT (poster + first caption):** 2–6 words that sharpen the spoken line (the number, the verdict, the stakes) — never a transcript of it.

**Order rule — SCENE BEFORE CLAIM.** When the hook turns on a physical object, place, or document, the scene is established *before or as* the claim lands — in the visual (frame 0 shows it) **and** in the words (the first line situates it: "The tablet flips toward you at the coffee counter…"). A claim about an unshown, unsituated object is an automatic fail, however strong the claim is.

## 3. Clarity rules (the cold-viewer laws)

1. **The 3-Second Stranger Test.** A stranger who sees frame 0 and hears only the first ~3 seconds must be able to answer: *What am I looking at? Where are we? Why does it matter / what's the question?* If any answer is missing, the open fails.
2. **Referent-before-use.** Every noun, name, and number is introduced before it is used. No referent may be *used* (pointed at, priced, accused) before it has been *shown or named in its scene*.
3. **The Demonstrative Test.** The words *that, this, it, they, the [noun]* in the first two lines are audited one by one: each must point at something already visible on screen or named in the same breath. "That 25% button" fails: *that* pointed at a button no one had been shown, in a place no one had been placed. (In the tip script the scene sentence existed — "The tablet flips around. The line behind you watches." — but it came *after* the claim. Order is the rule, not presence.)
4. **One idea per sentence; one sentence per breath.** If a sentence needs a comma splice to survive, split it. Numbers get their own beat: a figure the video turns on is never buried mid-sentence.
5. **No insider shorthand.** If a term needs the viewer's prior knowledge (a ticker, a form number, "the screen" without a scene), either situate it in ≤5 words or cut it.
6. **The mute test.** With sound off, frame 0 + poster text + the first two caption chunks must still deliver the scene and the question.
7. **The read-aloud test.** Every line must be speakable in one breath at narration pace (~0.33 s/word) without stumbling; tongue-twisters and stacked clauses are cut at script stage, not discovered at TTS stage.

## 4. Structure (the channel's 4-beat map, 35–55s)

1. **Scene + hook (0–~7s):** frame-0 scene; first line situates + lands the contradiction; second beat previews the payoff (what the viewer will know by the end).
2. **Mechanism (~7–25s):** how the thing actually works, in but/therefore steps. Every step is a new fact or a turn; no step restates the previous one.
3. **Proof (~25–45s):** the number, document, or moment that proves it — shown, not just said (the receipt math, the regulator's table, the filing). This is where the hook's loop closes.
4. **Verdict + loop (final ~5s):** the one-line takeaway that repays the opening, phrased to loop back into frame 0 (the closing line should make the first line hit harder on rewatch). No new claims in the verdict. No CTA inside the narration (subscribe lines are metadata's job, not the script's).

Re-hook rule: at each segment boundary there is a turn — a new question, a reversal, or an escalation of stakes — never a topic-change announced like a lecture ("Now let's talk about…").

## 5. The ten-pass review protocol (owner-mandated, 2026-11-15)

Every script — new or revised — goes through **ten review passes, each a distinct reviewer brief, before building**. Passes are run by different reviewers (separate AI reviewers where available; a pass may never be run by the script's author). Each pass produces: findings tied to line numbers, proposed rewrites, and a pass/fail verdict for its lens. The author then reconciles all ten into ONE revised script plus a change log (what each pass caught, what changed, what was rejected and why).

| # | Pass | The reviewer asks |
|---|------|-------------------|
| 1 | **Cold-Viewer Clarity** | §3, all seven laws. Stranger test on the first 3 seconds; every demonstrative audited; every referent introduced before use? |
| 2 | **Hook Strength** | §2. Zero-second hook? Shock + expectations + payoff preview? Works muted? Works as a title + thumbnail? Promise landed by ~2s? |
| 3 | **Fact Precision** | Every claim vs the sources file. Numbers re-computed. Dates/labels exact. No claim stronger than its source. (This pass holds a veto: a fact error blocks reconciliation until fixed.) |
| 4 | **Pace & Retention** | Beat map: a new idea/shift every sentence; no flat stretch >5s; re-hook at every segment boundary; payoffs land immediately after their setups; total runtime in band. |
| 5 | **Tab-Benchmark Taste** | Against Tab's best (Costco, Roadster, Roaring Kitty): a real moment? a person or object with emotional grip? Would a general audience share it? Passes the owner's appeal bar (broad shareability first)? |
| 6 | **Curiosity-Gap Integrity** | Every loop opened is identified, and closed at the promised point. The gap = the video's real value. The ending repays the opening (Hoyos debt rule). |
| 7 | **Visual-Script Alignment** | Every spoken claim has a showable visual; frame 0 = the hook's scene; no claim about an unseen object at any point in the script (not just the open); each segment's lead visual is named. |
| 8 | **Language & Readability** | 5th-grade ceiling; word economy (cut every word that earns nothing); read-aloud test; spoken numbers formatted as heard; no essay constructions. |
| 9 | **Structure & Story** | §4 map holds; but/therefore chaining; the turn exists; the verdict loops to frame 0; segments in the strongest order (is the best beat buried?). |
| 10 | **Hostile Pre-Mortem** | Assume the Short died at ~900 views like Odyssey/Pokémon. Write the autopsy in advance: the exact second a stranger swipes, and why. Re-check every unresolved flag from passes 1–9. Final line edit. |

**Reconciliation rules:** Pass 3 (facts) and Pass 1 (clarity) hold vetoes — punch never trades against either. Where passes conflict, the order of authority is: facts > clarity > hook > taste > style. The revised script keeps the channel format: 5 segments, one [HOLD] per segment, 140–165 spoken words, triple-hook spec rewritten to match the new opening, sources unchanged unless Pass 3 corrected them (corrections are logged).

## 6. Sign-off checklist (attach to every script before building)

- [ ] 3-Second Stranger Test passed by a reviewer who did not write the script
- [ ] Demonstrative audit clean (first two lines, word by word)
- [ ] Frame 0 shows the scene the first line names
- [ ] Payoff written before the hook; hook promises exactly that payoff
- [ ] Every loop opened is closed on screen; verdict repays the open
- [ ] Readability ≤ 5th grade; read-aloud clean
- [ ] Ten passes logged; reconciliation log complete; vetoes cleared
